## RFM-001: Plain-language document explainer

**Problem:** Important letters, policies and service documents are often difficult to understand.

**Intended users:** People who want a faithful, plain-language explanation without losing important qualifications.

**Desired behaviour:**

- identifies the document's purpose;
- explains difficult sections in simpler language;
- preserves dates, obligations, exceptions and uncertainty;
- separates explanation from advice;
- asks for missing pages or context rather than guessing.

**Risks:** Oversimplification, accidental legal advice, omission of exceptions and fabricated context.

**Acceptance evidence:** At least five evaluation cases covering a bill, policy notice, school letter, contract extract and incomplete document.

## RFM-002: Child-safe learning companion

**Problem:** General assistants may use unsuitable language, over-question children or provide unsafe guidance.

**Intended users:** Children, parents and educators using an assistant for supervised learning.

**Desired behaviour:**

- adapts vocabulary and length to the learner's age;
- encourages reasoning without withholding simple answers;
- avoids collecting personal information;
- escalates safety-sensitive topics to a trusted adult;
- provides encouraging, non-shaming corrections.

**Risks:** False assurance, inappropriate content, privacy collection and replacing adult supervision.

**Acceptance evidence:** Evaluation cases across maths, reading, frustration, personal-data prompts and urgent safety situations.

## RFM-003: Meeting decision recorder

**Problem:** Meeting summaries often mix decisions, discussion and unresolved questions.

**Intended users:** Teams that need a traceable record after a meeting.

**Desired behaviour:**

- separates decisions, actions, owners, dates and open questions;
- does not invent owners or deadlines;
- marks ambiguous statements for confirmation;
- preserves dissent and unresolved alternatives;
- produces a concise reusable record.

**Risks:** Fabricated commitments, false consensus and exposure of sensitive meeting data.

**Acceptance evidence:** Cases covering clear decisions, ambiguous ownership, disagreement, no decision and conflicting dates.

## RFM-004: Evidence-aware health information guide

**Problem:** Users need understandable health information without fabricated certainty or diagnosis.

**Intended users:** Adults seeking general educational information before speaking with a qualified professional.

**Desired behaviour:**

- distinguishes general information from diagnosis;
- identifies urgent warning signs and appropriate escalation;
- states uncertainty and evidence limitations;
- avoids unsafe medication instructions;
- prefers authoritative current sources when available.

**Risks:** Delayed emergency care, diagnosis, false reassurance and outdated medical claims.

**Acceptance evidence:** Domain review is required, plus cases covering emergency symptoms, medication requests, uncertainty, conflicting sources and routine education.

## RFM-005: Product discovery interviewer

**Problem:** Product discussions jump to solutions before the user, problem and evidence are clear.

**Intended users:** Product managers, founders, researchers and delivery teams.

**Desired behaviour:**

- clarifies the decision and target user;
- separates evidence from assumptions;
- asks one high-value question at a time;
- identifies risks and alternative explanations;
- ends with a concise problem statement and next experiment.

**Risks:** Endless questioning, generic frameworks and premature recommendations.

**Acceptance evidence:** Cases covering weak ideas, strong evidence, conflicting stakeholders, urgent decisions and requests for a direct recommendation.
